A callout sets some words apart in a box, as Notion draws one: an icon at the start of its first line, a fill in one of Notion's colours, and what it holds inside the box. Folded, it shows only its first line.

<Example name="callouts" />

## Try it

Choose Callout in the [slash menu](/editing/menus/), or type `> [!💡]` and a space at the start of a block. The words on its first line are its title; Enter starts a line of its body under it, inside the box. Press the icon's square to choose another icon, or no icon; press ⌘/ for the block's commands and choose a fill. Press ⌘Enter with the caret in it to fold it.

The box has a square of padding and Design system 1's corners, on a fill that is Notion's grey unless a colour is written. Its icon stands on the first square inside, the words from the next square; without an icon the words start on the first. The fills are Design system 1's pastels: grey, brown, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink and red, and white, the paper in a hairline frame. A product changes one with `--aa-callout-fill-<colour>`.

## The Markdown

| What you see | How you write it | What it stores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A callout | Callout in the slash menu, or `> [!💡]` and a space | `> [!💡] Title`, then `> The body` on each line under it |
| A callout in a colour | A fill in the block's commands (⌘/) | `> [!💡 yellow] Title` |
| A callout with a picture | A picture's address in the brackets | `> [!https://example.com/icon.png] Title` |
| A callout with no icon | No icon in the icon's choice | `> [!] Title` |
| A folded callout | ⌘Enter | `> [!💡]- Title` |
| A Markdown alert | `> [!note]` and a space | `> [!note] Title` |
| A toggle | Toggle in the slash menu | `> [!note]- Title` |
| A toggle heading | Toggle heading 1, 2 or 3 in the slash menu | `> [!note]- ## Title` |

The brackets hold the callout's icon, an emoji or a picture's `https://` address, and its colour, one of `gray`, `brown`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `blue`, `purple`, `pink`, `red` and `white`, in either order, either of them or neither. Without a colour the fill is grey; `gray` chosen from the commands is written, so a product reading the Markdown knows it was chosen. This is what Link writes for a Notion page's callouts, so a callout goes to Notion and back with its icon and colour.

## Markdown alerts

<Example name="callouts-alerts" />

A word in letters alone in the brackets is a Markdown alert, as GitHub and Obsidian write them: the type's icon on the first square, the words on the next and a hairline at the start, no box. Given an icon or a colour, an alert becomes a callout and its type word goes.

| Type | Icon |
| --- | --- |
| `[!tip]` | A tick |
| `[!warning]`, `[!caution]` | A box with a dot |
| `[!note]`, and any other | A box with lines |
| Any type, folded | A triangle |

Every line of a callout starts with `>`, so a Markdown reader that does not know callouts shows it as a quote.

## Folded callouts

<Example name="callouts-folded" />

A folded callout shows its title and hides its body. The body is kept as it was: folding and opening write or take away the one minus sign and nothing else. With the caret in a folded callout's body, the body is shown while it is edited. ⌘⌥T opens every folded callout in the editor. An alert folds from its icon too.

A toggle is a folded alert made from the slash menu, the same Markdown.

## Toggle headings

<Example name="toggle-headings" />

A toggle heading is a callout whose title is a heading, folded to begin with: `#`, `##` or `###` and a space after the type make the title one. The title is set in the headings' medium face, and it folds and opens as any callout does.

## Keys

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Enter | A new line of the body |
| ⌘Enter | Folds the callout the caret is in, or opens it |
| ⌘⌥T | Opens every folded callout and toggle in the editor |

From code, `editor.toggle()` folds or opens the callout the caret is in, or ticks the box it is in, and `editor.toggle(true)` opens a callout. `editor.callout({ icon, color })` gives the callout the caret is in (or the one at an index) another icon or colour, `null` taking the icon away or putting the grey back; `readCallout`, `writeCallout` and `setCallout` do the same on the Markdown. See [Methods and events](/building/methods/).
